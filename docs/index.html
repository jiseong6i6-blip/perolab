      Extrapolation notes

        How it is computed

        The three-layer drift-diffusion equations of IonMonger (Courtier et al., J. Comput. Electron. 2019) — electrons, holes, halide vacancies and potential — re-solved in Python and ported to JavaScript, running in a browser background thread (Web Worker). Same inputs give the same values as Python to three decimals. Temperature enters ion diffusion (Arrhenius), thermal voltage and intrinsic carrier density. One run takes 5–30 s.

        ERE (external radiative efficiency): the share of electron–hole recombination that leaves as light. Closer to 1 means less loss; record cells are about 0.01, ordinary cells 0.001. Wide-bandgap top cells are lower in practice.

        Remaining limitations

        • With the 'real data' basis, condition-group averages match within 1.5×, but individual cell lifetimes fall within 3× only about half the time because quality varies by lab.
        • More optimistic than the in-orbit measurement (SSPD-1). Cover-glass yellowing and pre-launch humidity exposure are not modelled.
        • Nitrogen and argon come out almost the same. Both are inert; in practice the remaining oxygen and moisture ppm matter far more than the gas.
        • The crystallisation constants (nucleation and growth) were fitted to typical results of four processes: antisolvent, gas, vacuum and no quench. Effects of spin speed, concentration, temperature and solvent come from property data and equations, but treat absolute values as ±50%.
        • The ion/hysteresis model is one-dimensional (through the film). Fast ion paths along grain boundaries are not included.
        • Activation energies of the degradation rates come mostly from 25–85 °C data. Outside that (−100 °C in space, 150 °C under concentration) the same laws are extended, so new reactions at those temperatures would be missed.
